The review comes first

A privacy review is the specification.

In this sector the hard requirements are written by someone who will never query the system. A reviewer decides which record may be read, by whom, for which purpose, and what may leave afterwards.

So the design starts from that document instead of from a dashboard. Consent, purpose and access are modelled before the first table exists, because a control added after the pipeline is a convention rather than a control.

Everything on this Page is one sentence made true in software: the analysis you want is the only thing the policy allows.

Where the boundaries go

Three copies of one patient, three rules.

Care

The record a clinician opens is identifiable, current, and traceable to the system it came from. This is the copy that has to be right first, because the others are derived from it.

Research

The cohort an analyst queries is a different object: pseudonymised, modelled for statistics, and held apart from the care copy by a rule rather than a habit. Identifiers are stripped before the record is mapped, and the mapping is a rule rather than a judgement.

What leaves

Between those two sits the environment where the work happens. What exits it is a result and the checks that produced it, which is the whole boundary described in one line.

Alignment, checked

EHDS is a test, not a paragraph.

We would rather show the checks than claim the compliance. Thirteen of them run against the platform's own catalogue, and they are deterministic on purpose: the same data gives the same verdict tomorrow, which is the only reason a check is worth citing.

What they look at is the boring half. Whether a dataset says what it may be used for and where its rows came from, in a form a review can be run against rather than read.
