# The site authors its own robots.txt, and states an AI stance on purpose

`https://www.datawego.nl/robots.txt` is generated by Cloudflare, not written
here. That has three costs, all verified: 33 `Disallow: /` blocks naming training
crawlers, and **no `Sitemap:` line at all**, so no crawler is told where the
sitemap is; a 119KB response, because the fallback for an unknown address (ADR 0008)
appends the whole homepage HTML behind the 2.7KB Cloudflare preamble; and a
`Disallow` for `GoogleOther`, the crawler Google uses for Discover, Ads and parts
of its AI features — blocking one of Google's own entrances while the rest of this
work tries to open another.

We decided the repository ships `dist/robots.txt` — `Allow: /`, plus
`Sitemap: https://www.datawego.nl/sitemap.xml` — and states the AI stance
deliberately instead of inheriting a default: **training crawlers refused,
retrieval and answer crawlers welcome** (`OAI-SearchBot`, `ChatGPT-User`,
`PerplexityBot`, `Applebot`), `GoogleOther` unblocked. A consultancy that sells AI
engineering capability has nothing to gain from its Dutch copy being training data
and something to lose from not existing inside AI answers.

The trap this decision exists to record: **`/docs/` is not disallowed.** Diagram
pages are kept out of the index by a `noindex` inside the page (ADR 0002), and a
`noindex` only counts if the crawler can fetch the page to read it. Blocking the
path in robots does the opposite of the intent — the URL stays linked from the
home Page, cannot be read, and can still be indexed as a bare entry with no
description. robots governs fetching; the meta tag governs keeping. Neither
substitutes for the other.

Consequence: the zone's Content-Signals managed-robots injection has to be turned
off, or the two files concatenate into the 119KB response this replaces.
